The value model

A pre-token protocol’s implied value is what its token would be worth if the market priced it the way it prices the protocols whose tokens already trade — fitted, not assumed. The curve is a log-log fit on the snapshot’s traded tokens, grouped into families so one token never counts twice, with small category, growth and seasoning tilts fitted by ridge regression. Exchange and bridge tokens are left out of the fit (their value does not relate to TVL the same way), and so are degenerate comps whose market cap is more than 10× their TVL.

Method & honesty notes

Coverage

The board ranks the 40 watchlist protocols, the above-floor pre-token protocols that pass the snapshot’s own legitimacy signals, and the largest below-floor pre-token protocols by TVL. A protocol is set aside, with its reason stated, when its category is a bridge, exchange or staking product, its name ends in a version marker or carries wrapped/staked, it begins with an exchange’s name, or it belongs to a multi-product family (two or more protocols sharing the same brand — the launch, if any, belongs to the family, not the deployment). No protocol name is ever special-cased.

What “pre-token” means here

Ranked by the snapshot’s record: a protocol the snapshot records with no token. The snapshot is DefiLlama’s record, not proof a token does not exist — a protocol may have a token its record does not carry, and this board never claims otherwise. Values are estimates from a fitted curve, not predictions that a token launches or when.

Farm economics

Airdrop pool: 5% of the implied value, the conservative end of the usual 5–15% community airdrop. Participation: 20% of TVL actively farms points; the rest is passive, so the slice is the deposit’s share of the farming fifth. Horizon: 26 weeks, 3 hours of attention (1 hour to set up, about 5 minutes a week). Costs: gas ($5 on a cheap chain, $25 on Ethereum mainnet) plus the 4% T-bill return the deposit gives up. Verdict: FARM when the net pay beats $5/hour and the break-even odds sit under 50%; SKIP when the net pay is negative or the break-even odds pass 100%; THIN in between.

The $1,000 tier decides the board’s verdict; the detail view shows the same plan sized to $100 and $10,000. The biggest launches are the worst $1,000 farms — a small deposit is a rounding error in a billion-dollar pool — so the best farms sit further down the board, where the slice is a real share of a smaller pool.
